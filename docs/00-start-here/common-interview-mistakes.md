# Common Interview Mistakes

This page collects mistakes that commonly cost experienced candidates senior-level offers.

Many of these are not knowledge gaps. They are communication, judgment, and process mistakes.

## Communication mistakes

- Starting with a long preamble instead of a direct answer.
- Explaining everything at maximum depth without reading the interviewer.
- Going silent while thinking, hiding your reasoning.
- Using jargon without showing you understand it.
- Not asking clarifying questions on open-ended problems.

## Technical answer mistakes

- Naming tools instead of explaining trade-offs.
- Giving memorized definitions with no practical context.
- Ignoring failure modes, edge cases, and production concerns.
- Treating one pattern as universally correct.
- Defending the first idea instead of comparing alternatives.

## System design mistakes

- Jumping to a solution before clarifying requirements and scale.
- Designing for scale that the problem does not require.
- Skipping data flow, consistency, and failure handling.
- Forgetting non-functional concerns: monitoring, cost, and rollback.
- Going deep on one component and never covering the whole system.

## Behavioral mistakes

- Vague stories with no concrete actions or outcomes.
- Taking full credit for team work, or blaming others.
- No examples of ownership, conflict, or learning from failure.
- Answers that do not show impact or measurable results.

## Process and mindset mistakes

- Bluffing when you do not know something.
- Arguing with the interviewer instead of collaborating.
- Over-optimizing the first answer and running out of time.
- Not verifying claims ("this is faster") with reasoning or metrics.
- Preparing only theory and skipping mock interviews.

## How to avoid them

- Practice answering out loud, not just reading.
- Record yourself or do mock interviews to catch habits.
- Always pair a claim with a trade-off or a reason.
- Prepare concrete stories before behavioral rounds.
- Treat the interview as a collaboration, not an exam.
