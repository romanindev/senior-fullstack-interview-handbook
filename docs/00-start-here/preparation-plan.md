# Preparation Plan

This page describes a structured way to prepare for senior-level interviews.

The goal is not to study everything, but to prepare deliberately across the areas that senior interviews actually test.

## What senior interviews test

Most senior full stack loops cover four areas:

- Core engineering knowledge (React, TypeScript, Node.js, fundamentals)
- System design and architecture
- Practical problem solving and debugging
- Behavioral and leadership signals

A good plan gives each area attention instead of over-preparing one and ignoring the rest.

## A four-week baseline plan

Adjust the timeline to your situation. The order matters more than the exact dates.

### Week 1: Fundamentals and gaps

- List the technologies in the job description.
- Rate your confidence in each from 1 to 5.
- Study the lowest-rated topics first.
- Rebuild mental models, not memorized definitions.

### Week 2: Depth in core topics

- Go deep on React, TypeScript, and Node.js internals you are weak on.
- For each topic, be able to explain what problem it solves and its trade-offs.
- Practice explaining topics out loud in two minutes.

### Week 3: System design and architecture

- Practice designing common systems end to end.
- Focus on requirements, trade-offs, data flow, and failure modes.
- Practice drawing simple diagrams while talking.

### Week 4: Behavioral, mock interviews, and review

- Prepare concrete stories about ownership, conflict, and incidents.
- Do timed mock interviews, ideally with another engineer.
- Review weak areas surfaced by the mocks.

## How to split your time

A balanced split for most candidates:

- 40% core engineering topics
- 30% system design
- 20% behavioral and communication
- 10% coding and debugging practice

Shift the ratio toward whichever area your target loop emphasizes.

## Daily practice loop

For each study session:

1. Pick one topic or question.
2. Answer or explain it without notes first.
3. Compare with a strong structured answer.
4. Note the gap.
5. Re-explain it out loud.

## Signs you are ready

- You can give a clear short answer, then add depth on request.
- You reason about trade-offs instead of naming tools.
- You can design a basic system and defend your choices.
- You have concrete stories ready for behavioral questions.
- You stay calm when you do not know something.
